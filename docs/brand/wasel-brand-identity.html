01 — Market read

Why the current palette can't stay

Ride-hailing in the MENA region has effectively standardised on green. Careem, Bolt and inDrive all lead with it. Wasel Captain's current primary — #047857 in light, #34D399 in dark — sits inside that crowd rather than beside it.

A real collision, not a matter of taste

In driver_palette.dart the dark-mode brand primary is #34D399. In alert_tokens.dart the availability token onlineDark is also #34D399 — byte-identical. On the Captain's home screen the brand colour and the "you are online" signal are the same green, so the single most important piece of state in the whole app carries no colour meaning of its own. Moving the Captain brand off green fixes this at the root.

The second problem is that Rider indigo and Captain green share no visual DNA. They read as two unrelated products rather than two halves of one network. The proposal below gives both apps a common spine and lets each lead with a different colour from it.

02 — The two identities

Velocity Teal and Signal Amber

One shared teal spine. The Rider app leads with it; the Captain app carries it as a secondary and leads with amber instead. Same family, opposite temperature — which is exactly the difference between the two jobs.

Cool, bright, in motion. Teal is unclaimed in this category, survives on both light and dark map tiles, and pairs with a violet accent for premium tiers and promotions.

The reasoning, colour by colour

  • Teal for the passenger. Blue is the trust colour every fintech and airline already uses, and it disappears into map water and route lines. Teal keeps blue's calm-and-competent read but shifts far enough toward cyan to stay legible over both light and dark cartography. It is also the closest colour to the brand's own meaning — واصل, to connect and to arrive.
  • Violet as the Rider accent. Reserved for surge, premium tiers and promotional surfaces. Violet reads as "elevated" without reading as "alarm", which red and orange both do — and those two hues must stay free for error and warning semantics.
  • Amber for the driver. Two independent reasons converge. Perceptually: warm, long-wavelength light at low luminance is the standard for night instrumentation because it disturbs dark adaptation least, and the Captain app is looked at repeatedly in a dark cabin. Culturally: amber is taxi. A driver recognises the category instantly.
  • Amber with ink on top, never white. White text on amber is unreadable. Every amber surface carries #1A1206 ink — measured at 10.13:1 in dark mode. This is the same convention as a taxi roof sign, and it makes the Captain's primary CTA the highest-contrast element on the screen.
  • Green freed for status. With the Captain brand off green, statusOnline green finally means one thing only: you are online and earning. Amber and green are far enough apart in hue that the distinction survives at a glance, in peripheral vision, while driving.
  • Graphite neutrals, not blue-grey. The Captain's neutral ramp is hue-neutral graphite so the amber never picks up a competing cast. Rider neutrals carry a faint cyan bias so its surfaces feel of a piece with the teal rather than pasted onto default grey.

How trust is actually built here

Colour alone does not make a rider feel safe; consistency and legibility do. Three rules carry most of the weight. One brand colour means one thing — teal is always "this commits you", never decoration, so a rider learns in two trips exactly which control charges their card. Status colour is never brand colour — the green/amber/red band belongs to state alone, which is what makes "driver arriving" and "trip cancelled" readable without reading. And every foreground pair clears WCAG AA, so the interface never asks a stressed passenger at night, or a driver in direct sun, to work at reading it.

Three new tokens, and why they're worth it

primaryVivid separates the colour the brand expresses with from the colour it acts with — the electric cyan and the taxi amber are too light to carry white text, but they're the memorable part of each identity, so they get a home instead of being smuggled in as raw hex. borderStrong exists because neutral200 is a decorative divider at roughly 1.3:1 and is currently doing double duty as the input outline, which fails the 3:1 non-text requirement in both apps today. onPrimaryVivid guarantees an amber fill never picks up a white label by accident.

04 — Typography

One family, two voices

The recommendation keeps the single-family discipline but changes the family: Readex Pro, which was drawn from the start as a matched Arabic-and-Latin pair rather than a Latin face with Arabic bolted on.   • Weights ship at 300–700. Readex Pro is variable; restrict usage to 400 / 500 / 600 / 700 so the ramp stays legible and the download stays small.
  • Readex Pro has no tabular figures — confirmed, and it needs a decision. Inspecting the shipped TTFs: the GSUB feature list is calt ccmp dnom fina frac init liga locl medi numr rlig — no tnum — and the digit advances are proportional (Latin 0–9 run 500–620 units, Arabic-Indic 281–630). FontFeature.tabularFigures() would be a silent no-op, so a ticking fare or trip timer will visibly shift. Three ways out: give numeric styles a second family that does carry tnum, lay ticking numbers out in fixed-width slots, or accept the shift outside the live-trip screens. Flagged rather than chosen — it changes the type system.
  • Arabic needs more leading than Latin. Arabic ascenders and descenders run deeper. Set body line-height to 1.7 rather than the 1.5 Latin default, or diacritics clip.
  • Hierarchy by weight and size only — never by colour. Colour carries state in this system; if it also carried emphasis, neither would read cleanly.
  • The switch has a runtime cost. google_fonts fetches faces over the network on first launch. Bundling Readex Pro as a local asset instead removes a cold-start dependency and a first-paint flash — recommended for both apps regardless of which family wins.
05 — Shape & dimension

Soft for the rider, squared for the captain

Shape is where the two apps separate most cheaply. Rider rounds generously — pills and 20px cards read approachable and consumer. Captain squares off — a 14px radius reads as a control rather than a suggestion, and drivers are operating equipment, not browsing.

This needs one architectural change

AppDimens is currently a static class shared by both apps, so it can't express a per-brand radius. The clean fix is an AppShape record resolved from AppBrand exactly as the palette already is — brand.shapeFor() alongside the existing brand.paletteFor(brightness) — and AppTheme.build() reads shape from the brand rather than from a global. Spacing, icon sizes and the 4/8 rhythm stay shared and stay in AppDimens.

Elevation and motion, briefly

  • Keep elevation at zero, keep the borders. The current theme already sets elevation: 0 everywhere and separates surfaces with colour and hairlines. That's the right call for a map-heavy app — drop shadows over cartography read as mud. No change.
  • Two motion tokens, not one. fast at 160ms for press and state feedback, standard at 260ms with an emphasised-decelerate curve for sheets and route arrivals. Exits run at roughly 70% of the entrance.
  • The Captain app moves less. Anything animating in the driver's peripheral vision competes with the road. Restrict motion there to state feedback and sheet transitions — no decorative reveals, no staggered lists.
  • Respect reduced motion in both. Query MediaQuery.disableAnimations and collapse transitions to crossfades.
06 — Tech debt

The duplication is already gone

Correction to the brief

The brief describes ~175 lines duplicated between light() and dark() in packages/core/lib/theme/app_theme.dart. That's no longer the state of the file. It is 224 lines total, and both methods are already one-line delegates into a single build({required brand, required brightness}). It looks like the recent architecture-and-performance audit (99865b2) collapsed it. There is no duplication left to remove — the goal named in the brief is complete.

So the theme work in this proposal is not a deduplication. It's a re-palette of a structure that's already correct, plus three additions the new system needs. What genuinely remains:

  • The palette files carry the duplication now, not the theme. rider_palette.dart and driver_palette.dart each repeat the same 18 AlertTokens.* assignments across their light and dark constants — four near-identical blocks. A const AppColorsExtension.withAlerts({...}) factory that fills the alert band from the shared ramp would remove about 70 lines and make it impossible to wire a status token to the wrong brightness.
  • Input borders fail non-text contrast in both apps today. inputDecorationTheme uses neutral200 for enabledBorder, which measures roughly 1.3:1 against the field fill. borderStrong fixes it.
  • Shape can't vary per brand. Covered above — AppShape resolved from AppBrand.
  • Nothing else blocks the rebrand. A sweep found zero raw Color(0x…) literals anywhere outside lib/theme/, across both apps and all packages. All 284 colour reads go through context.colors.* and all 190 text reads through context.styles.*.

Which makes this an unusually cheap rebrand

Because the token layer is airtight, swapping the two palette files re-skins every screen in both apps with no call-site edits at all. The risk in this project isn't the repaint — it's the two map style JSONs in assets/map_styles/, which hold their own colours outside the token system and have to be retuned by hand to sit under the new palettes.

07 — On approval

How this lands, in order

Sequenced so the app compiles and runs at every step, and so the visible change lands in one reviewable commit rather than trickling across the codebase.

  1. Extend the token contract ✓ DONE

    Add primaryVivid, onPrimaryVivid and borderStrong to AppColorsExtension, including copyWith and lerp. Add the withAlerts factory in the same pass. Both palettes still hold their current values — nothing changes visually yet.

  2. Repaint the palettes ✓ DONE

    Replace the four constants in rider_palette.dart and driver_palette.dart with the values specified above. This is the commit where both apps change appearance. It touches two files.

  3. Introduce per-brand shape NOT STARTED

    Add AppShape and brand.shapeFor() beside the existing paletteFor(). Thread it through AppTheme.build() so buttons, cards and sheets read radii from the brand. Point inputDecorationTheme at borderStrong in the same change.

  4. Swap the type family and split the ramp FAMILY DONE · RAMP PENDING

    Move AppTextStyles and AppTheme's textTheme from IBM Plex Sans Arabic to Readex Pro, apply the per-app ramp, and add tabular figures to the numeric styles. Bundle the face locally rather than fetching it at runtime.

  5. Retune the map styles NOT STARTED

    Rewrite assets/map_styles/light.json and dark.json against the new grounds, and check the route stroke — #097A8F on light cartography, #5FE2FA on dark — actually holds 3:1 over real tiles rather than over a flat swatch.

  6. Verify on device

    Both apps, both themes, RTL, largest Dynamic Type. Then /flutter-code-review for the layer-boundary and theming rules, and @code-reviewer before anything opens as a PR.

Two calls I'd like made before step one

First, the Captain amber — it's the boldest move here and it walks away from a green the drivers may already associate with the brand. Second, the type change, which is the only item with a real runtime cost; keeping IBM Plex Sans Arabic and adopting only the per-app ramp is a perfectly good outcome if you'd rather not move the family. Everything else follows from the tables above.
